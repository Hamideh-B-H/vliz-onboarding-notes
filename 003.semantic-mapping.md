# Semantic mapping

Semantic mapping is the process of translating raw, unstructured, or tabular data (like CSVs, SQL databases, or Excel sheets) into a standardized graph structure—like the RDF/SOSA format in your all-triples.ttl file.

Instead of just converting file formats, semantic mapping **gives raw data explicit, universal meaning** by linking every column and row to shared definitions (ontologies).

## The Problem: Raw Data Has No Context

Imagine your lab stores water measurements in a standard CSV file:

| **Sample_ID** | **Temp** | **DO** |
|---|---|---|
| BPNS_11 | 18.5 | 7.19 |

To a human, you might guess what this means. But to a computer or another lab's system:

- What does DO mean? (Dissolved Oxygen? Digital Output? Delivery Order?)
- What unit is 18.5 in? (Celsius? Fahrenheit?)
- What is BPNS_11? (A location? A sample ID? A device code?)

## The Solution: Semantic Mapping

Semantic mapping defines a set of **rules** that translate every piece of that CSV table into explicit RDF triples using established ontologies like SOSA and QUDT:

```
"Column Sample_ID" ---> sosa:Sample
"Column Temp" ---> sosa:observedProperty <.../sea_water_temperature>
"Column DO" ---> sosa:observedProperty <.../dissolved_oxygen>
"Value 7.19" ---> qudt:numericValue "7.19"
```

After applying the semantic map, your raw CSV row becomes the precise graph statement from your file:

```turtle
<.../EMOBON_BPNS_So_11#diss_oxygen> a sosa:Observation ;
sosa:observedProperty <.../dissolved_oxygen> ;
sosa:hasResult [
qudt:numericValue "7.19"
] .
```

## Why Semantic Mapping Matters (What to tell your manager)

1. **Interoperability:** It allows data from completely different software, databases, or research partners to be merged seamlessly because everyone maps to the same vocabulary (SOSA/QUDT).
2. **Machine-Readability:** Computers can automatically reason across datasets, search for specific oceanographic properties, and execute SPARQL queries without manual data cleaning.
3. **No Schema Lock-in:** You don't have to rewrite your existing relational databases; you simply apply a semantic mapping layer over top of your existing data pipelines.
